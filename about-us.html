What Is Organic Food?

Organic food, sometimes called ecological or biological food, is food and drink produced under the rules of organic farming. The exact standards differ from country to country, but they share the same aims: recycle resources on the farm, keep the ecosystem in balance and conserve biodiversity.

Organic rules limit which pesticides and fertilizers a farmer may use. Organic products are also generally processed without irradiation, industrial solvents or synthetic food additives, which makes them a natural fit for anyone looking for clean label food and fewer ultra-processed ingredients.

What this means on our farm

  • No synthetic pesticides or chemical fertilizers, so our crops are grown as pesticide-free food.
  • Only non-GMO seed varieties.
  • Compost, crop rotation and cover crops to feed the soil.

Why Certified Organic Food Matters

Today the European Union, the United States, Canada, Mexico, Japan and many other countries require producers to earn special certification before they can sell food as organic. Vegetables from a home garden may well be grown organically, but only certified producers may put the organic label on food they sell.

That label is regulated by government food-safety authorities, such as the USDA National Organic Program in the United States and the European Commission in the EU. FarmFresh is inspected every year, so the certified organic food you buy from us is independently verified, from seed to shelf.

Organic Food Benefits for the Environment

In conventional farming, heavy fertilizing, overproduction and pesticide use can harm ecosystems, soil health, biodiversity, groundwater and drinking water. Organic farming is designed to reduce or avoid these problems.

Regenerative agriculture at FarmFresh

We go a step further with regenerative agriculture: minimal tillage, year-round ground cover and hedgerows that shelter pollinators. Healthy soil stores more carbon and holds more water, and it grows more nutrient-dense food.
